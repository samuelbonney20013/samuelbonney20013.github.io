Graduate Thesis

Nonparametric Tilted Density Estimation and Bickel–Rosenblatt Goodness-of-Fit Testing

My graduate thesis investigates tilted kernel density estimators and their use in goodness-of-fit testing.

The project focuses on selecting bandwidth and probability weights using cross-validation-based procedures and evaluating the resulting estimators through extensive simulation studies.

Current Focus

The work examines:

  • Tilted kernel density estimation
  • Cross-validation-based bandwidth selection
  • Probability-weight selection
  • Bickel–Rosenblatt goodness-of-fit testing
  • Asymptotic behavior
  • Empirical test size
  • Statistical power
  • Simulation-based performance evaluation
  • Comparison with conventional kernel density estimators

The broader goal is to understand whether tilted density estimators can improve inferential performance relative to conventional kernel-based approaches under different data-generating settings.

County-Level Lung Cancer Mortality in the United States

Social Vulnerability, Behavioral Risk Factors, Spatial Clustering, and Unexplained Mortality Hotspots

This project examines geographic variation in lung cancer mortality across U.S. counties.

I integrated data from the CDC Social Vulnerability Index, CDC PLACES, and the National Cancer Institute for 2,536 counties and applied a combination of classical regression, spatial statistics, machine learning, and mediation analysis.

Methods

  • Robust Ordinary Least Squares regression
  • Quantile regression
  • Global Moran’s I
  • Local Indicators of Spatial Association
  • Spatial error models
  • Random Forest
  • XGBoost
  • SHAP interpretation
  • Mediation analysis
  • Geographic visualization

Key Findings

The analysis identified 232 unexplained high-mortality spatial clusters after accounting for observed socioeconomic and behavioral factors.

The study also estimated that COPD mediated approximately 50.8% of the county-level association between smoking and lung cancer mortality.

County-Level Food Insecurity and Chronic Disease Burden

This project evaluates the joint geographic burden of food insecurity and chronic disease across U.S. counties.

The analysis included 2,299 counties and combined food insecurity, social-needs, and chronic-disease data.

Methods

  • Construction of a standardized disease-burden index
  • Multivariable regression
  • Sensitivity analysis
  • Spatial autocorrelation
  • Geographic mapping

Key Finding

The analysis identified 408 counties experiencing both high food insecurity and high chronic disease burden, highlighting potential priority areas for public-health intervention.

BioMed-Scribe

Medical Specialty Classification from Clinical Conversations

BioMed-Scribe is a large language model project focused on classifying clinical conversations into medical specialties.

I fine-tuned GPT-2 Medium using a two-block parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy on 13,492 clinical conversations.

Model Performance

The final model achieved:

  • 79.1% test accuracy
  • 0.799 macro-F1
  • Classification across Cardiology, Neurology, Orthopedics, and General Medicine

Methods and Tools

  • GPT-2 Medium
  • Parameter-efficient fine-tuning
  • Clinical text preprocessing
  • t-SNE embedding analysis
  • Model evaluation
  • Gradio application deployment

The project demonstrates how large language models can be adapted to specialized clinical classification tasks while limiting the number of trainable parameters.

Statistical Analysis of Diabetes Risk Factors

This project investigates clinical, behavioral, demographic, and socioeconomic factors associated with diabetes-related outcomes among American adults.

The analysis used 253,680 observations from the CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System.

Methods

  • Data transformation
  • Chi-square tests
  • Factorial ANOVA
  • Multiple regression
  • Logistic regression
  • Model selection
  • Regression diagnostics
  • Sex-stratified analysis

The analysis was conducted using both SAS and R.

Modeling Life Expectancy in Africa

This project used socioeconomic and health indicators to model life expectancy across African countries.

A multiple linear regression model was developed to identify key predictors of national life expectancy.

The final model achieved an adjusted R-squared of approximately 0.98, with adult mortality and HIV incidence emerging as major predictors.

Malaria Research in Ghana

My earlier research focused on the use of machine learning to study malaria risk in Ghana.

Malaria Among Pregnant Women

Using Demographic and Health Survey data, I applied explainable machine learning techniques to predict malaria risk among pregnant women.

The K-Nearest Neighbor model achieved the strongest predictive performance, with an AUC of approximately 0.88.

The analysis also ranked important predictors such as age and anaemia status.

Undergraduate Thesis

My undergraduate thesis examined machine learning approaches for identifying risk factors associated with malaria incidence among children under five years of age in Ghana.

The project evaluated multiple machine learning algorithms, including:

  • Random Forest
  • Neural Networks
  • K-Nearest Neighbors
  • Additional classification models

The analysis used data from the 2019 Ghana Malaria Indicator Survey.

Fractal Pattern Generation via Iterated Function Systems

This project explored computational applications of linear algebra through the generation of fractal patterns.

I developed a Python program that applied affine transformations iteratively to geometric shapes using matrix operations and translation vectors.
